### 1. Exact physical/normalized commutator

For

\[
T(x)=x\oplus((2x)\lor(4x)),\qquad A(x)=T(x)\gg2,
\]

put \(y=T(x)\). Then

\[
\boxed{A(Tx)=T(Ax)\oplus c(y)}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{c(y)=(y_0\lor y_1)+2(y_1\land\neg y_2)}.
\]

Thus the failure of physical and normalized evolution to commute is supported only in the two low bits and takes values in \(\{0,1,3\}\).

This is the structural reason a near-return defect cannot be propagated through physical time using the XOR mask alone: the required source information has been discarded by normalization.

## Dead route closed

Do not pursue an argument of the form

\[
\text{boundary mask }1\text{ or }3
\Longrightarrow
\text{persistent defect/debt}
\Longrightarrow
\text{future surplus}.
\]

Both allowed masks can be erased in one physical step, and identical masks can evolve differently in different phases.

Also avoid treating a physical near-return as if

\[
A^p(x)=\left\lfloor T^p(x)/2^{2p}\right\rfloor
\]

for arbitrary \(x\). Repeated normalization discards source bits that can affect later boundary evolution; the exact commutator above is the correct local bookkeeping device.
