# Problem 1: naturality of the boundary-to-diagonal endpoint map

## Setup

At fixed ambient period `p`, let `T_p(a,b)=(y,a)` on the off-zero domain `a != 0`, where `y` is the unique solution of

`b = S y XOR (a OR y)`.

For nonzero `x`, define `rho_p(x)=d` when the boundary connector beginning at `(x,0)` first reaches the diagonal `(d,d)`. Run 80 proves this return exists; run 81 proves `rho_p` is a bijection.

## Theorem 1: rotation equivariance

For every cyclic shift `S^k`,

`rho_p(S^k x) = S^k rho_p(x)`.

### Proof

The inverse equation is translation-equivariant. If `y` solves

`b = S y XOR (a OR y)`,

then `S^k y` solves the same equation for `(S^k a,S^k b)`. Uniqueness therefore gives

`T_p(S^k a,S^k b) = (S^k y,S^k a)`.

Thus applying `S^k` to every word in the connector from `(x,0)` produces exactly the connector from `(S^k x,0)`. Its first diagonal hit is the shifted first diagonal hit, proving the claim.

Consequently `rho_p` descends to a bijection on nonzero necklaces.

## Theorem 2: exact repetition compatibility

Let `q` divide `p`, let `m=p/q`, and write `R_m(u)=u^m` for repetition of a `q`-bit word into ambient period `p`. Then for every nonzero `u`,

`rho_p(R_m(u)) = R_m(rho_q(u))`.

Moreover, the entire boundary-to-diagonal connector at period `p` is exactly the repeated period-`q` connector and has the same number of `T`-steps.

### Proof

Suppose `(a,b)` is a pair of repeated words,

`a=R_m(a0)`, `b=R_m(b0)`.

If `y0` is the unique period-`q` predecessor satisfying

`b0 = S y0 XOR (a0 OR y0)`,

then `R_m(y0)` satisfies the period-`p` equation because cyclic shift, OR, and XOR all commute with repetition:

`R_m(b0) = S R_m(y0) XOR (R_m(a0) OR R_m(y0))`.

Since `a != 0`, predecessor uniqueness at ambient period `p` forces the period-`p` predecessor to be exactly `R_m(y0)`. Hence the repeated-word subset is invariant under every deterministic off-zero step and

`T_p(R_m(a0),R_m(b0)) = (R_m(y0),R_m(a0))`.

Starting from `(R_m(u),0)=R_m((u,0))`, induction shows that every pair on the period-`p` connector is the repetition of the corresponding period-`q` pair. Equality of the two components is also preserved and reflected by repetition, so the first diagonal hit occurs at exactly the same step. Its endpoint is `R_m(rho_q(u))`.

## Dyadic corollary

For doubling,

`rho_{2p}(uu) = rho_p(u) rho_p(u)`.

Thus the restriction of `rho_{2p}` to the inherited period-`p` subspace is not merely related to `rho_p`: it is exactly a conjugate copy of `rho_p` under word doubling. No long period-`2p` traversal is needed for any repeated boundary state.

In particular, any genuinely new difficulty in passing from `p` to `2p` is confined to exact-period-`2p` boundary words (including the antiperiodic integrations generated by doubled odd leaves). This matches the earlier inherited-subgraph/leaf-portal decomposition from the zero-return graph side.

## Additional consequence: preservation of exact rotational period

Because `rho_p` is rotation-equivariant and bijective, it preserves stabilizers under the cyclic rotation action. Indeed, if `S^k x=x`, equivariance gives `S^k rho_p(x)=rho_p(x)`. Conversely, if `S^k rho_p(x)=rho_p(x)`, apply equivariance to `rho_p(S^k x)=S^k rho_p(x)=rho_p(x)` and use injectivity of `rho_p` to obtain `S^k x=x`.

Therefore

`per(rho_p(x)) = per(x)`

for every nonzero word `x`.

This is stronger than repetition compatibility: the endpoint matching never changes the exact rotational period of a boundary word.

## Research significance

The endpoint bijection is block-diagonal by exact rotational period and is equivariant under rotations. At dyadic `p`, all proper-period sectors are inherited recursively from lower periods. Therefore the unknown part of `rho_p` lives entirely on primitive/full-period necklaces. For the p=32 portal problem, the relevant antiperiodic boundary words are exact period 32, so the remaining target is to understand `rho_32` on that primitive sector rather than the full `2^32-1` boundary set.
