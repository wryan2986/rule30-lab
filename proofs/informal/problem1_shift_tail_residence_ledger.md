# Zero-extension excess is exactly long-residence surplus minus skipped characteristics

Status: `partial-proof` for the exact ledger and the periodic-source two-step lemma below. This sharpens the scalar excess target from `problem1_shift_tail_excess_reduction.md`; it does **not** prove the required surplus is unbounded and does not close Problem 1. No numerical experiment or finite census is used.

## 0. Purpose

After the finite right support ends, the previous note reduces the late bounded-strip alternative to boundedness above of

    e_v(n) = tau(2^n v) - n.

Its increment form

    e_v(N) = tau(v) + sum_(n<N)(delta_v(n)-1),
    delta_v(n)=tau(2^(n+1)v)-tau(2^n v),

is exact, but still mixes three geometrically different cases. Since zero-extension preperiods are nondecreasing, every `delta_v(n)` is a nonnegative integer. Splitting the sum therefore gives an exact amortized ledger: skipped front characteristics are the only negative units, one-step residences are neutral, and long residences supply the only positive units.

This is useful because the existing clock-doubling theorem already identifies a distinguished infinite family of skipped characteristics. Any proof from clock growth must now show how the positive long-residence surplus outruns **all** skips, rather than merely produce infinitely many long residences.

## 1. Exact skip/surplus decomposition

Put

    h_n = tau(2^n v),
    delta_n = h_(n+1)-h_n >= 0.

For N>=0 define

    Z_v(N) = #{0<=n<N : delta_n=0},

and

    P_v(N) = sum_(0<=n<N, delta_n>=2) (delta_n-1).

Terms with `delta_n=1` are deliberately absent. For every n,

    delta_n-1 =
      -1                  if delta_n=0,
       0                  if delta_n=1,
       delta_n-1          if delta_n>=2.

Hence exactly

    e_v(N) = tau(v) + P_v(N) - Z_v(N).              (1)

Consequently

    limsup_N e_v(N)=infinity

is equivalent to

    limsup_N [P_v(N)-Z_v(N)] = infinity.            (2)

Likewise, boundedness above of the zero-extension excess is equivalent to boundedness above of the surplus-minus-skip ledger.

No density statement is hidden in (1). Infinitely many positive increments do not suffice: a long residence of length `ell` contributes only `ell-1`, while each skip consumes one unit. Conversely, arbitrarily large individual residence lengths would suffice, but no such theorem is currently available.

## 2. Exact global-front meaning after finite support

Use the fixed finite original row and notation of `problem1_global_discrepancy_front.md`. Let R end the actual right support and set

    v=L_R(r)>0,
    s_j=tau(L_j(r)).

For j=R+n,

    s_(R+n)=tau(2^n v)=h_n.

The complete residence interval of characteristic j is

    [s_(j-1),s_j) intersect Z,

so the residence length of characteristic `R+n+1` is exactly

    s_(R+n+1)-s_(R+n)
      = h_(n+1)-h_n
      = delta_n.                                    (3)

Thus the three cases in Section 1 have literal front geometry:

- `delta_n=0`: characteristic `R+n+1` is skipped by the global front;
- `delta_n=1`: it is visited for exactly one physical time and is ledger-neutral;
- `delta_n>=2`: it has a long residence and contributes exactly `delta_n-1` units of positive surplus.

Therefore (1) may be read without any scalar abstraction:

> zero-extension excess = initial delay + cumulative long-residence surplus - cumulative skipped characteristics.

The already proved implication

    p_j=2 p_(j-1)  =>  s_j=s_(j-1)

shows that every clock-doubling characteristic in the tail contributes one member of `Z_v`. The converse is not claimed: the present results do not show that every skipped characteristic is a clock doubling.

## 3. What an eventual bounded strip forces

If the original finite row has an eventual physical delay bound K, the prior exact tail identity gives eventually

    e_v(N) <= R+K.

Combining with (1), eventually

    P_v(N) - Z_v(N) <= R+K-tau(v).                  (4)

Thus a bounded strip has a precise amortized interpretation: all positive excess generated by long front residences can be paid for, up to one fixed additive constant, by skipped characteristics.

This exposes two proof strategies and their exact burden:

1. prove the long-residence surplus `P_v` exceeds the total skip count `Z_v` by arbitrarily large amounts on the FULL finite-fringe domain; or
2. prove a charging theorem in which skips can pay for only a bounded amount of future long-residence surplus, with infinitely much surplus left uncharged.

Charging only the known clock-doubling subset of skips is insufficient unless one also controls non-doubling skips. Similarly, proving infinitely many `delta_n>=2` is insufficient unless their total `(delta_n-1)` surplus beats `Z_v`.

## 4. A local compensation lemma at a purely periodic doubling source

There is one exact place where a clock-doubling skip can already be paired with the following extension. This does not cover the eventual nonperiodic tail, but it clarifies why a naive one-event argument nearly works and exactly where it stops.

Let y be a positive finite A-periodic state and suppose z=2y is also A-periodic with doubled least period. The established doubling classification says the low A-trace of y is identically zero. As in `problem1_highest_wait_nonforcing.md` Section 1, write

    b_s = bit_0(A^s z).

The doubling forces periodic b to contain both 0 and 1. For w_s, the low trace of 2z=4y, the driven recurrence is

    w_(s+1)=b_s OR w_s,   w_0=0.                    (5)

Its unique recurrent response is identically 1. Let

    q=min{s>=0:b_s=1}.

Since z=2y is even, `b_0=0`, hence q>=1. Equation (5) gives

    w_s=0 for 0<=s<=q,
    w_s=1 for s>=q+1.

The projected state z is periodic from time 0, so the only transient in 2z is this driven low bit. Therefore

    tau(4y)=q+1 >= 2.                                (6)

In particular, for the two zero extensions beginning at this purely periodic doubling source,

    [tau(2y)-tau(y)-1]
      + [tau(4y)-tau(2y)-1]
      = tau(4y)-2 >= 0.                              (7)

So the `-1` ledger unit created by the doubling skip is recovered by the very next extension in the purely periodic case.

There is a small strengthening for a genuine deep zero-extension tower. If y=2^n v with n>=2, then y_0=y_1=0. Using `A=sigma^2 T` and `T(x)=x XOR ((x<<1) OR (x<<2))`, one gets

    b_1=bit_0(A(2y))=y_1 XOR y_0=0.

Hence q>=2 and

    tau(4y)>=3,                                      (8)

so the two-step ledger contribution in (7) is at least +1.

The limitation is essential: after a zero-extension tower has acquired a positive inherited transient, its states are no longer A-periodic, and (5)-(8) cannot simply be applied at time zero. The existing monotonicity means such a tower never returns to preperiod zero. A proof for the actual late tail must therefore transport an analogous compensation statement through the inherited transient, or use the global residence ledger directly.

## 5. Current target and stopping fence

The exact target is now (2), not merely divergence of `tau(2^n v)` and not merely infinitely many positive renewal injections. The most promising structural question is:

> On the FULL finite-fringe domain, can every skipped characteristic be matched against later long-residence surplus in a way that forces `P_v(N)-Z_v(N)` to have unbounded positive excursions?

The periodic-source lemma shows such compensation locally before any inherited transient appears, but it does not justify discarding that transient. Future work should test whether the one-bit eraser history admits a **transient-aware** version of (7). If it does not, an exact countermechanism showing how inherited transients allow skips to keep paying all long-residence surplus would close this route cleanly.

Do not resume finite sampling to estimate an average drift. Equations (1)-(4) are exact and already identify the required all-depth imbalance.
