# Problem 1: boundary defects can re-enter immediately; exact physical/normalized commutator

Status: proved identities + explicit counterexamples. Problem 1 remains open.

## Setup

Use the packed Rule-30 map

\[
T(x)=x\oplus((2x)\lor(4x)),\qquad A(x)=T(x)\gg2.
\]

The preceding return-fringe analysis shows that when a long physical persistence block first fails, the defect is extremely small: for the first failing row \(w\),

\[
A^p(w)=w\oplus\varepsilon,\qquad \varepsilon\in\{1,3\}.
\]

A natural hope was that this low-end defect might persist, grow monotonically, or act as a debt that must later be repaid. That hope is false in its simplest form.

## 1. Exact one-step commutator

Let

\[
y=T(x),\qquad q=A(x)=y\gg2.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{A(Tx)=T(Ax)\oplus c(y)}
\]

where the correction mask is supported only in the two lowest bits and is

\[
\boxed{
 c(y)=(y_0\lor y_1)+2\bigl(y_1\land\neg y_2\bigr)
}
\]

so \(c(y)\in\{0,1,3\}\).

### Proof

For Rule 30 in packed coordinates,

\[
T(y)_i=y_i\oplus(y_{i-1}\lor y_{i-2}),
\]

with negative-index bits zero.

For every \(i\ge2\),

\[
A(Tx)_i=T(y)_{i+2}
=y_{i+2}\oplus(y_{i+1}\lor y_i)
=T(q)_i,
\]

because \(q_i=y_{i+2}\). Hence the two sides can differ only in bits 0 and 1.

At bit 0,

\[
A(Tx)_0=y_2\oplus(y_1\lor y_0),
\qquad
T(Ax)_0=y_2,
\]

so the correction bit is \(y_0\lor y_1\).

At bit 1,

\[
A(Tx)_1=y_3\oplus(y_2\lor y_1),
\qquad
T(Ax)_1=y_3\oplus y_2,
\]

whose XOR is \(y_1\land\neg y_2\). This proves the formula.

This identity makes precise why a defect under \(A^p\) cannot in general be pushed one physical step forward by applying \(T\) to the defect alone: normalized evolution and physical evolution fail to commute by a source term depending on the low bits discarded by normalization.

## 2. A one-bit boundary defect can vanish on the next physical step

Take

\[
z=28519.
\]

It has exact \(A\)-period \(p=4\), and

\[
T^4(z)=2^8z+23.
\]

Thus the return fringe is \(R=23\), with

\[
G=8-\operatorname{bitlength}(23)=3,
\qquad
D=\lfloor G/2\rfloor=1.
\]

The first row after the guaranteed persistence block is

\[
w=T^{D+1}(z)=T^2(z)=455503.
\]

As already observed in the boundary-collision note,

\[
A^4(w)=w\oplus1.
\]

But one physical step later,

\[
T(w)=1645809
\]

and direct exact iteration gives

\[
\boxed{A^4(Tw)=Tw}.
\]

So an allowed boundary defect \(1\) can disappear completely after one physical Rule-30 step.

The re-entry is not permanent. Continuing from \(w\), the defect masks

\[
E_j:=A^4(T^jw)\oplus T^jw
\]

begin

\[
\boxed{1,\ 0,\ 1,\ 3,\ 10,\ldots}.
\]

Thus even exact re-entry to the same \(A^p\)-fixed set need not persist.

## 3. A three-bit boundary defect can also vanish immediately

There is an equally explicit example for the other allowed collision mask. Take

\[
z=205726.
\]

It has exact \(A\)-period \(p=4\), and

\[
T^4(z)=2^8z+62.
\]

Here

\[
G=8-\operatorname{bitlength}(62)=2,
\qquad D=1.
\]

The first failing physical row is

\[
w=T^2(z)=3282734.
\]

Direct exact iteration gives

\[
\boxed{A^4(w)=w\oplus3}.
\]

Yet

\[
T(w)=14576082
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{A^4(Tw)=Tw}.
\]

Therefore the \(3\)-defect can also be erased on the immediately following physical step.

Again this is only transient. The defect masks from \(w\) begin

\[
\boxed{3,\ 0,\ 3,\ 1,\ 2,\ldots}.
\]

## 4. The defect mask alone does not determine the next behavior

The preceding examples are not merely isolated re-entry phenomena. They show that the mask \(\varepsilon\in\{1,3\}\) is not a sufficient state variable.

For comparison, the canonical period-two examples behave differently:

- \(w=401\) has \(A^2(w)=w\oplus1\), but after one physical step the defect is
  \[
  A^2(Tw)\oplus Tw=4,
  \]
  not zero.
- \(w=802\) has \(A^2(w)=w\oplus3\), but after one physical step the defect is
  \[
  A^2(Tw)\oplus Tw=9.
  \]

Thus both collision masks admit qualitatively different continuations: immediate re-entry in some physical phases and immediate spreading in others.

## Consequence for the proof strategy

The route

> long neutral plateau \(\to\) boundary mask \(1\) or \(3\) \(\to\) persistent defect/debt \(\to\) later residence surplus

is invalid without additional phase/source information.

The exact commutator identifies what is missing. To transport a near-return through physical time one must retain at least the low source bits generated by the intermediate physical row; the XOR defect by itself forgets precisely the data appearing in

\[
c(Tx)=(T(x)_0\lor T(x)_1)+2(T(x)_1\land\neg T(x)_2).
\]

A useful next target is therefore a finite-state description of a \(p\)-step near-return augmented by the source-bit/phase data needed to evolve it under one physical \(T\)-step. The goal would be to determine whether repeated long return-fringe corridors force a non-reusable event in that augmented automaton. Any argument using only the scalar defect mask is ruled out by the counterexamples above.
