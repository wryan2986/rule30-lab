finite-capacity reduction. The same-cell charge is `refuted` on the general

## 0. Admission and exact question

The round-ten renewal requires infinitely many resetting injections on ONE
hypothetical FULL finite-entry orbit. Round303 route 2 proposed charging
those events to P_n(Y_0,s), with s<n and bounded multiplicity. Before
attempting that global charge, locate the ACTUAL 1 which erases each
injection. If its pullback lies inside the original anchored window, this
would supply a candidate assignment. If it does not, determine how much
transport is missing. Both outcomes bear directly on the whole-tail route.

This is the separate anchored route, following the round304 refutation of
finite-support parity rigidity. No birth, source, clock, nilpotent-word or
prefix search is admitted. Rankings remain `heuristic`: exact index audit
first, then global ancestry with controlled reuse; extending a finite local
observation is not an alternative after the round303 no-go.

The generic ray bridge and cycle-delay renewal are established imports.
The new work applies them to the precise inherited-horizon reset, calculates
its sharp distance from the WHOLE anchored sampling domain, and separates
per-ray capacity from total capacity after one actual finite-entry rebase.

## 1. The erasing 1 lies on the newly attached characteristic (`partial-proof`)

Keep one actual Rule 30 spacetime r_i(u), including its complete fixed initial
right fringe. Put Y_t=sum_(j>=0) r_(-j)(t)2^j. Assume every Y_t is eventually
A-periodic, with tau_t its least preperiod. The existing renewal defines

    T_t=max(tau_t-1,0),
    R_t=tau_(t+1)-T_t >=0.

If R_t>0, let rho_t=R_t-1 and lambda_t=tau_(t+1)=T_t+rho_t+1.
The periodic upper row is q_t=A^(T_t+1)Y_t. Its first low 1 occurs at
A-time rho_t. Therefore

    bit_0(A^k Y_t)=0 for T_t+1 <= k < lambda_t,
    bit_0(A^lambda_t Y_t)=1.                         (1)

When rho_t=0 the zero interval is empty. The physical diagonal identity
sigma^k Y_(t+k)=A^k Y_t turns (1) into

    r_(-k)(t+k)=0 for T_t+1 <= k < lambda_t,
    r_(-lambda_t)(t+lambda_t)=1.                    (2)

Thus the erasing 1 has exact physical location

    E_t=(i,u)=(-lambda_t, t+lambda_t), lambda_t>=1.  (3)

Define the characteristic coordinate chi=i+u. This entire waiting word
0^(R_t-1)1 has chi=t. Increasing the inherited delay or the waiting time
moves the event along that SAME characteristic; it never creates an
original positive ray depth.

For comparison, the incorrect residual bit is evaluated at

    (-T_t, t+1+T_t), with chi=t+1.

This is the actual bit e_t in the renewal, not necessarily a nonzero bit.
At the final waiting update it is erased by the adjacent characteristic's
1 in (3). No earlier transient is identified with the phase-correct lift.

A second coordinate derivation starts directly from the one-bit scalar
recurrence for Y_(t+1)=2AY_t+c_(t+1). At scan time s, its upper low driver is
bit_0(A^(s+1)Y_t). At the last disagreement s=T_t+rho_t=lambda_t-1,
that driver is the 1 at (3). The subsequent low-bit update is at
(-lambda_t,t+1+lambda_t). This independently checks both time offsets.

## 2. Sharp distance from original anchored observations (`partial-proof`)

The original anchored activity samples physical cells

    (i,u)=(-2n-s-epsilon,s),
    n>=1, 0<=s<n, epsilon in {0,1}.                 (4)

Call this sampling domain D. Activity is NOT required in the definition
of D; the active cells form a subset. Every cell in D has
chi=-2n-epsilon<=-2, whereas every reset (3) has chi=t>=0.
Thus a reset is never itself an original anchored observation, including
t=0. Formal pullback would require bit depth -t. At even t=2m the
reset lies in the depth-zero pair of the initial cut W_m at A-time
2m+lambda_t; pulling that pair back m times would require pair depth -m.
Negative depths are not defined for the original one-sided Y_0.

The separation is quantitatively sharp. With ordinary physical spacetime
L1 distance, for ALL integers t>=0 and lambda>=1,

    dist_1((-lambda,t+lambda), D)
        = t + ceil(2(lambda+1)/3).                 (5)

Proof of lower bound. Write j=2n+epsilon and U=t+lambda. For a point
(-j-s,s) in D, let d be its L1 distance from (-lambda,U). The sum
and difference of the coordinate differences give

    d >= t+j,
    d >= t+2lambda-j-2s >= t+2lambda-2j+2,          (6)

since 2s<=2n-2<=j-2. Consequently

    d-t >= max(j,2lambda-2j+2) >= 2(lambda+1)/3.

Integrality proves the lower bound in (5). For attainment choose:

| lambda | n | s | epsilon | attained distance minus t |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3k, k>=1 | k | k-1 | 1 | 2k+1 |
| 3k+1, k>=0 | k+1 | k | 0 | 2k+2 |
| 3k+2, k>=0 | k+1 | k | 0 | 2k+2 |

Each choice has n>=1 and 0<=s<n. Substitution gives the displayed
distance, equal to the ceiling in (5). This is an all-integer proof,
not a finite distance census.

In particular every charge of a reset's erasing 1 to an ORIGINAL active
anchored cell must displace it by at least t+2. A bounded physical
neighborhood of that 1 cannot supply such a target at arbitrarily late
reset times. The equality in (5) concerns D; a nearest sample may be
inactive, so equality is not asserted for the active subset.

The existing finite 55 example gives a nonvacuous check of the same-cell
failure. With its zero right fringe, Y_0=55 is cyclic, Y_1=100 is cyclic,
and Y_2=223 has least preperiod1. Thus R_1=1, lambda_1=1, and the
reset is (-1,2). Its distance to D is 3, attained at (-2,0), an active
cell of 55. This uses the existing closed cycles and the actual odd-row
cyclicity theorem; no new orbit extension is performed.

Quantifier fence: we have NOT refuted every FULL-conditioned local-charge
lemma. If no FULL finite-entry orbit exists, such a lemma may be vacuous.
What (5) proves is that ON ANY hypothetical such orbit, its infinitely
many required resets could not be matched to original observations within
a uniform physical radius. A proof of a uniformly local charge under FULL
would therefore itself yield a contradiction and must use more than the
imported coordinate identities. Global, distance-growing transport remains
unexcluded and unconstructed.

## 3. A supremum budget is not a total activity budget (`partial-proof`)

Write J_n(x)=sum_(s=0..n-1)P_n(x,s) and Q(x)=sup_(n>=1)J_n(x).
On the full finite-entry domain, Q(x)<infinity does NOT bound

    sum_(n>=1) J_n(x).

For example A(-1)=0. Every initial pair is nonzero and every later pair
zero, so J_n(-1)=1 for all n>=1, Q(-1)=1, but the sum is infinite.
This is a hand counterexample to a budget inference, not a FULL survivor.
Thus a bounded-multiplicity assignment to arbitrary original rays would
not, by itself, bound the total number of resets from Q.

There is a precise repair using ONE actual rebase. Suppose the fixed
complete initial right fringe is finite and Q(Y_0)<=K. Put

    h=h_J(K),  H=2 ceil(h/2).

The established anchored finite-entry theorem gives A^hY_0 finite and
bitlen(A^hY_0)<=2(K+h+1). A preserves positive finite bit length and
fixes zero, so the same bound holds for A^H Y_0. The physical bridge gives

    sigma^H Y_H=A^H Y_0,
    bitlen(Y_H)<=H+2(K+h+1).                        (7)

Restoring H low bits is harmless also when the projected row is zero.
The entire row at physical time H is finite: its left half is finite by
(7), and the finite original right fringe has a finite rightward cone.
Rebase once at H and RETAIN that entire actual right fringe. Under FULL,
H is even so the center phase is still 1,0,1,0,... . Set x=Y_H.
The exact finite-input Q formula gives

    q=Q(x) <= K+h+H/2 =: K'.                        (8)

For any finite x, all P_n(x,s) vanish above the highest pair, whose index
is q (with q=0 when there is no pair of index >=1). Since J_n<=n,

    #{(n,s):n>=1, 0<=s<n, P_n(x,s)=1}
        = sum_(n>=1)J_n(x) <= q(q+1)/2
        <= K'(K'+1)/2.                             (9)

This is now a genuine FINITE target set. The rebase is fixed once;
it is not repeated at later resets. No subsequent fringe is reinitialized.

## 4. The exact remaining charge obligation (`inconclusive`)

For this one rebased row x and its retained actual finite right fringe,
the renewal and clock-growth theorem still force infinitely many R_t>0
under FULL. A constructive assignment of all sufficiently late resets to
the active PAIR samples in (9), with each pair sample receiving at most
some finite C charges, would give

    number of charged resets <= C K'(K'+1)/2,

and a contradiction. C may depend on the one initial configuration; it
must not grow with the observation horizon. If cells rather than pair
samples are the targets, the corresponding capacity has an extra factor
of at most two. No assignment or bound C is supplied here.

Equation (5), applied with the rebased physical origin, shows why the reset
1 itself and every uniformly bounded physical neighborhood are insufficient:
the required displacement grows at least linearly in the event index.
Tracing to some original ancestor is also not enough; reuse of a fixed
ancestor must be bounded, and the chosen ancestor must lie in the specific
anchored domain rather than merely in the finite initial support.

The next valid step is therefore a GLOBAL construction with controlled
reuse on this same original row, or a different use of the complete
nilpotent phase history. Do not repeat the generic ray shift, compute more
reset positions, or enlarge local neighborhoods to refit the index failure.
